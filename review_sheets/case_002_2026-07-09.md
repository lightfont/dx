### Fluid status assessment (for hyponatraemia workup)  ·  `pe07`  ·  cost: free  ·  relevant, sig-neg
- **Finding:** Skin turgor normal. Mucous membranes moist. No peripheral oedema (no ankle or sacral oedema). JVP not elevated. No orthostatic hypotension. Clinical assessment: EUVOLAEMIC — neither dehydrated nor fluid overloaded.

### Urine Osmolality & Sodium  ·  `bi05`  ·  cost: low  ·  relevant
- **Finding:** Serum osmolality: 258 mOsm/kg (low — normal 275–295 mOsm/kg). ## Key teaching points
1. Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) is an aggressive lung cancer strongly associated with heavy smoking. It arises centrally, near the main airways, and is typically already widespread at diagnosis. It is the lung cancer most strongly associated with paraneoplastic syndromes.
   _Clinician: ☐ accurate  ☐ needs change_
2. Paraneoplastic syndromes occur when tumour cells produce substances that affect other organs — without cancer having directly spread there. SCLC is strongly associated with SIADH via ectopic ADH production.
   _Clinician: ☐ accurate  ☐ needs change_
3. SIADH causes euvolaemic hyponatraemia. The kidneys retain excess water, diluting the blood sodium. When sodium falls below approximately 125 mmol/L, brain swelling causes confusion. The diagnostic criteria for SIADH are: low serum sodium + low serum osmolality + inappropriately concentrated urine + high urine sodium (>40 mmol/L) + euvolaemia + exclusion of adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism, and diuretics.
   _Clinician: ☐ accurate  ☐ needs change_
4. In euvolaemic hyponatraemia, always consider Addison's disease (adrenal insufficiency) as an important and potentially life-threatening cause, as it can closely mimic SIADH biochemically. A 9 am serum cortisol should be checked early, and a Synacthen (ACTH stimulation) test is often used to confirm the diagnosis when appropriate. It is essential to exclude adrenal insufficiency before attributing hyponatraemia to SIADH, as it is immediately treatable with steroid replacement. Particularly, Addisonian crisis should not be missed, and steroid replacement in this condition can be rapidly life-saving.
   _Clinician: ☐ accurate  ☐ needs change_
5. TB should be considered in patients presenting with chronic cough, haemoptysis, weight loss, and systemic symptoms, particularly in high-risk or endemic settings. In suspected cases, appropriate infection-control precautions and isolation should be initiated early until tuberculosis is confirmed or confidently excluded, to reduce the risk of transmission.
   _Clinician: ☐ accurate  ☐ needs change_
6. Cardiac tamponade is a must-not-miss in any patient with lung cancer and progressive dyspnoea — lung cancer extends to the pericardium more commonly than any other primary cancer. Beck's triad (raised JVP, muffled heart sounds, hypotension) with pulsus paradoxus identifies it clinically; echocardiogram confirms.
   _Clinician: ☐ accurate  ☐ needs change_
7. When a patient presents with two seemingly unrelated problems (breathlessness AND confusion), always ask whether one diagnosis can explain both. In SCLC, the cancer explains breathlessness (tumour mass, pleural effusion) AND confusion (paraneoplastic SIADH → hyponatraemia). The hyponatraemia is a clue pointing to the lungs, not the diagnosis itself.

## Approach insight
This case illustrates the principle of 'one diagnosis explaining two problems.' The SCLC explains both the breathlessness (tumour mass + pleural effusion) AND the confusion (paraneoplastic SIADH → hyponatraemia → cerebral oedema). The confusion is the diagnostic clue — it should prompt the question 'why is this patient confused?' which leads to serum electrolytes, which reveals the hyponatraemia, which triggers the SIADH workup, which points back to the lung. The sodium level is not the diagnosis; it is the pointer.

## Expert narrative
SCLC presenting with paraneoplastic SIADH is a classic advanced presentation. The central reasoning: (1) A heavy smoker with 6 weeks of progressive cough, haemoptysis, weight loss, night sweats, and new confusion has SCLC with paraneoplastic SIADH until proven otherwise. (2) The confusion triggers electrolyte testing, which reveals severe hyponatraemia. (3) Euvolaemia on examination localises this to SIADH (not dehydration, not cardiac or hepatic failure). (4) Addison's and hypothyroidism must be actively excluded before diagnosing SIADH — they are life-threatening and immediately reversible. (5) The CXR confirms a central hilar mass, and bronchoscopy biopsy provides histological diagnosis. (6) Treatment of the underlying SCLC is the primary treatment for the SIADH.

## Sensitivity note
SCLC has poor sensitivity for 'classic' respiratory symptoms — it frequently presents with weight loss, hyponatraemia, confusion, or neurological symptoms before obvious breathlessness develops. Paraneoplastic syndromes may precede diagnosis. Any heavy smoker with constitutional symptoms and unexplained confusion warrants urgent chest imaging and electrolyte testing.

## Specificity note
Tissue biopsy (bronchoscopy or CT-guided) with SCLC histology (small cells with high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio, neuroendocrine immunohistochemistry — TTF-1, synaptophysin, CD56 positive) combined with a central lung mass on CT is highly specific. SIADH diagnosis requires: hypo-osmolar hyponatraemia + inappropriately concentrated urine + urine sodium >40 mmol/L + euvolaemia + exclusion of Addison's, hypothyroidism, and diuretic use.
