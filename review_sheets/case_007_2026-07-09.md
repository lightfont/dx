## Approach
The first fork in any tremor is rest versus action. A rest tremor that is asymmetrical, with bradykinesia and rigidity, is parkinsonism — and the task is then to separate idiopathic Parkinson's disease from the reversible mimics (drugs), the treatable mimics (NPH, Wilson's) and the atypical Parkinson-plus syndromes.

## Approach insight
The danger in tremor is not that Parkinson's disease is hard to spot — it usually isn't — but that the treatable and reversible causes get skipped because the picture 'looks like PD'. So the discipline is to actively exclude them: review the drug list (drug-induced parkinsonism is reversible and the commonest mimic), check thyroid function and, in the young, copper studies, and keep normal-pressure hydrocephalus in mind when gait and continence dominate. Asymmetry, a rest tremor, hyposmia and REM sleep behaviour disorder all build the case for idiopathic PD, while early falls, a vertical gaze palsy or early autonomic failure pull towards a Parkinson-plus syndrome. The DaTscan earns its place by separating degenerative parkinsonism from essential tremor and drug-induced disease — but it cannot, by itself, tell PD apart from Parkinson-plus.

## Expert narrative
Seeing an older man with an asymmetrical rest tremor, the neurologist is thinking Parkinson's disease immediately — but spends the consultation proving it is idiopathic and not one of the impostors. The examination confirms parkinsonism (bradykinesia with decrement, cogwheel rigidity, rest tremor) and then deliberately hunts the red flags: vertical gaze (PSP), a postural blood-pressure drop (MSA), early falls, early dementia. The history mines for supportive non-motor clues (hyposmia, dream enactment, constipation) and, crucially, for a culprit drug — because drug-induced parkinsonism is reversible and missing it is the real error. Bloods screen the reversible mimics, a DaTscan separates degenerative parkinsonism from essential tremor and drug-induced disease, and a clear levodopa response seals an idiopathic PD diagnosis. The skill rewarded here is not pattern-spotting PD, but systematically retiring the treatable causes before committing.

## Sensitivity note
The clinical MDS criteria are sensitive for parkinsonism, and a DaTscan is highly sensitive for the dopaminergic deficit of degenerative parkinsonism — but a normal DaTscan does not exclude essential tremor or drug-induced parkinsonism (indeed it supports them). Non-motor features (hyposmia, RBD) increase diagnostic confidence but are not individually sensitive.

## Specificity note
Asymmetry, a rest tremor and an excellent levodopa response are relatively specific for idiopathic PD. A DaTscan is specific for separating degenerative parkinsonism from essential/drug-induced tremor, but is NOT specific for PD versus Parkinson-plus (both reduce uptake). Vertical supranuclear gaze palsy is specific for PSP, and a normal scan with a clear culprit drug is specific for drug-induced parkinsonism.
