**Ventricular Tachycardia** — Excluded
  - _hx_: No syncope or pre-syncope and no family history of sudden cardiac death
  - _inv_: ECG shows a regular narrow-complex sinus tachycardia, not a broad-complex tachycardia

**Myocardial Infarction** — Ruled out
  - _hx_: No exertional or rest chest pain
  - _inv_: ECG shows no ischaemic changes and high-sensitivity troponin is normal

**Thyroid Storm** — Not in storm
  - _ex_: No hyperpyrexia, no delirium/agitation beyond mild anxiety, and no heart failure (a low Burch-Wartofsky score)
  - _inv_: Thyrotoxic biochemistry but without the decompensation that defines storm

**Phaeochromocytoma** — Ruled out
  - _hx_: No paroxysmal headache–palpitation–sweating crises with severe hypertension
  - _inv_: Plasma free metanephrines normal

**Atrial Fibrillation** — Ruled out

## Approach
Palpitations are triaged by first excluding the dangerous cardiac causes (ventricular tachycardia, ACS, AF with rapid rate) with an ECG, then working through the systemic drivers — endocrine (thyrotoxicosis, phaeochromocytoma, hypoglycaemia), haematological (anaemia) and psychogenic (anxiety) — and finally stimulant/drug effects.

## Approach insight
The discipline in palpitations is to clear the dangerous arrhythmias before chasing the endocrine story, even when the endocrine story is loud. An ECG up front retires ventricular tachycardia and confirms whether the rhythm is sinus or atrial fibrillation, and a troponin de-escalates ACS. Only then is the rich thyrotoxic picture assembled — weight loss with a good appetite, heat intolerance, tremor, a diffuse goitre with a bruit, eye signs and pretibial myxoedema — and the key intellectual step is not stopping at 'thyrotoxic' but going on to prove the cause: TRAb and a diffuse uptake pattern nail Graves', distinguishing it from toxic nodules and from the low-uptake thyroiditis that is managed entirely differently. Anxiety, anaemia, hypoglycaemia, stimulant use and phaeochromocytoma are the adrenergic mimics held in mind and retired in turn.

## Expert narrative
A young woman with weeks of palpitations gets an ECG before anything else — not because thyrotoxicosis isn't obvious once you look, but because ventricular tachycardia and ACS do not forgive being deferred. With a regular sinus tachycardia and a normal troponin, the clinician then reads the thyrotoxic signature that the vignette deliberately withheld: the weight loss with preserved appetite, the heat intolerance and tremor, and on examination the diffuse vascular goitre, the exophthalmos and lid lag, the pretibial myxoedema. Thyroid function confirms thyrotoxicosis, but the expert does not stop there — TRAb and a radioiodine uptake scan are used to prove Graves' specifically, because the management of Graves', a toxic nodule and thyroiditis diverge. Throughout, the adrenergic mimics — anxiety, anaemia, hypoglycaemia, phaeochromocytoma, caffeine — are each actively excluded rather than assumed away.

## Sensitivity note
A suppressed TSH is the most sensitive single screen for thyrotoxicosis; free T4 and free T3 confirm and grade it. TRAb is sensitive for Graves' (the great majority are positive), and a radioiodine uptake scan reliably separates the high-uptake causes from low-uptake thyroiditis. The ECG is sensitive for the dangerous arrhythmias at the moment of recording but may miss paroxysmal events, which is why the rhythm history matters.

## Specificity note
Eye signs, pretibial myxoedema and a positive TRAb are highly specific for Graves' disease. A diffusely increased radioiodine uptake is specific for Graves' versus toxic nodules (focal uptake) and thyroiditis (low uptake). A regular pulse with sinus rhythm on ECG is specific against atrial fibrillation, and normal metanephrines specifically exclude phaeochromocytoma.
