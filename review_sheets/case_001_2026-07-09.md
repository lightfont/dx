## Must-not-miss rule-out reasoning
**Myocardial Infarction** — Confirmed — this is the diagnosis
  - _hx_: Squeezing epigastric pain radiating to jaw and left shoulder, with sweating and nausea — classic features of inferior MI
  - _hx_: Crescendo pattern: 2 similar episodes in past week, each resolving spontaneously — unstable angina progressing to MI
  - _hx_: Loaded cardiovascular risk profile: hypertension, diabetes, 30 pack-year smoking, father died of MI at 58
  - _inv_: ECG: ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF with reciprocal depression in I and aVL — diagnostic of Inferior STEMI
  - _inv_: Troponin 10 times above normal, rising significantly at 3 hours — confirms ongoing heart muscle death

**Aortic Dissection** — Ruled out
  - _hx_: No sudden tearing onset, no radiation to the back between the shoulder blades — both features of dissection are absent
  - _ex_: Equal blood pressures in both arms (R: 158/96, L: 156/94 — difference only 2 mmHg). Dissection typically causes >20 mmHg difference
  - _ex_: All peripheral pulses equal bilaterally — no evidence of blood vessel compression from a dissection

**Aortic Aneurysm / Ruptured AAA** — Ruled out
  - _ex_: No pulsatile mass felt in the abdomen — a large AAA may be palpable
  - _ex_: Haemodynamically stable (blood pressure maintained) — ruptured AAA typically causes circulatory collapse
  - _ex_: Equal peripheral pulses — no evidence of iliac artery involvement from an expanding aneurysm

**Mesenteric Ischaemia** — Largely excluded
  - _hx_: No history of atrial fibrillation, recent heart attack, or severe hardening of arteries — the usual causes of bowel ischaemia
  - _ex_: Soft abdomen without peritonism. Mesenteric ischaemia classically causes 'pain out of proportion to the examination', but frank peritonism develops later as bowel dies
  - _inv_: Lactate 1.9 mmol/L (borderline normal). Severe mesenteric ischaemia typically causes lactic acidosis with lactate well above 2.0 mmol/L

**Perforated Viscus** — Largely excluded
  - _ex_: Soft abdomen with no peritonism (no guarding, rigidity, or rebound tenderness) — a perforated viscus causes peritonitis with marked guarding and a rigid abdomen
  - _inv_: Erect chest X-ray shows no free air under the diaphragm — pneumoperitoneum would be expected with a perforated viscus

## Key teaching points
1. Inferior MI frequently presents as epigastric (upper abdominal) pain rather than chest pain. Referred pain from the inferior-wall myocardium travels via autonomic (vagal) afferents and can be perceived in the epigastrium. ## Approach
A systematic approach to abdominal pain organises causes by mechanism, ensuring you consider dangerous diagnoses that might not initially come to mind:

## Approach insight
The VASCULAR/REFERRED category is the most commonly missed in clinical practice. Inferior MI belongs here — it causes epigastric pain not because the stomach is damaged, but because the pain signals from the lower heart wall travel through the same spinal nerves (T6-T10) as signals from the stomach. Using a systematic approach forces you to consider this category even when the symptom looks entirely GI.

## Expert narrative
Inferior STEMI presenting as epigastric pain is the most important diagnostic pitfall in emergency medicine. The right coronary artery (RCA) supplies the lower (inferior) wall of the left ventricle, the right ventricle, and the heart's natural pacemaker. Blockage explains all the features: epigastric pain (shared T6-T10 nerve supply), sweating and nausea (vagal activation), and slow heart rate (pacemaker ischaemia). Never let the location of pain determine what organ you think about — in any patient over 40 with cardiovascular risk factors and upper abdominal discomfort, obtain an ECG immediately.

## Sensitivity note
Inferior MI is poorly sensitive for 'classic' central chest pain — only about 40-50% of inferior MIs present this way. The rest present as epigastric pain, nausea, jaw pain, or arm pain. This means you must use a highly sensitive screening test (ECG) broadly for any patient over 40 with unexplained upper abdominal symptoms.

## Specificity note
ST elevation in leads II, III, and aVF with reciprocal changes in I and aVL is highly SPECIFIC for inferior STEMI (~95%). Once this ECG pattern is seen, the diagnosis is established with high confidence — competing diagnoses do not need further investigation before starting treatment.
