## Approach
Ptosis and diplopia can arise anywhere from muscle to brainstem. The discriminating question is whether the weakness is fatigable (worse with use, better with rest) and whether the pupil and reflexes are spared — features that localise to the neuromuscular junction and, specifically, to myasthenia gravis.

## Approach insight
The trap in this presentation is to localise too soon. A drooping lid and double vision instinctively suggest a third-nerve palsy or a stroke, and both are dangerous — but the moment the history reveals that the deficit fluctuates and fatigues, the lesion is pulled down to the neuromuscular junction. Two bedside negatives then do most of the discriminating work: a spared pupil takes a compressive third-nerve palsy and botulism off the table, and preserved reflexes separate MG from Guillain–Barré and Lambert-Eaton. What must never be deferred is the airway: fatigable bulbar and respiratory muscles are the reason MG is a must-not-miss, so vital capacity is measured early and tracked, long before any antibody result returns.

## Expert narrative
An experienced clinician hearing 'droopy lid and double vision, worse in the evening' is already thinking neuromuscular junction before the patient finishes the sentence — the diurnal, fatigable quality is that specific. The examination is then used to confirm fatigability (sustained upgaze, Cogan's twitch, the ice-pack test) and to harvest the negatives that exclude the dangerous mimics: a normal pupil, preserved reflexes, intact sensation. Crucially, the airway is assessed in the same breath as the eyes — a single-breath count and a formal FVC — because the patient who looks comfortable can still be sliding towards a crisis, and SpO2 will not warn you until it is late. Only then does the work-up move to the confirmatory layer: anti-AChR antibodies (anti-MuSK if negative), repetitive nerve stimulation or single-fibre EMG, and CT of the thorax to look for a thymoma. The discipline is to hold stroke, Guillain–Barré and botulism live until a specific finding retires each, rather than collapsing onto MG because it is the expected answer.

## Sensitivity note
Anti-AChR antibodies are ~80–85% sensitive in generalised MG but under 50% in purely ocular MG, so a negative result does not exclude the diagnosis — anti-MuSK antibodies and single-fibre EMG (the most sensitive test, ~95–99%) extend the work-up. Bedside FVC is the most sensitive practical marker of early respiratory compromise and is far more reliable than waiting for oxygen desaturation.

## Specificity note
Fatigable ptosis with a spared pupil and preserved reflexes is highly specific for a neuromuscular junction disorder, and a positive anti-AChR antibody (specificity >98%) effectively seals MG. A >10% decrement on repetitive nerve stimulation is specific for impaired neuromuscular transmission, while an incremental (facilitating) response instead points to Lambert-Eaton syndrome or botulism.
