## Approach
A subacute limb deficit in a young adult is localised by tempo and by level: days-not-seconds points away from stroke, and the presence or absence of a sensory level decides how urgently the cord must be imaged. If the picture is multifocal and demyelinating, the task becomes proving dissemination in space and time while excluding the compressive, vascular and nutritional mimics.

## Approach insight
Two early decisions shape this case: the tempo and the level. A deficit that evolves over days rather than seconds steers away from stroke, and the search for a discrete sensory level decides whether the cord must be imaged urgently for compression. From there the work is to assemble a multifocal CNS picture — optic neuropathy, internuclear ophthalmoplegia, pyramidal and cerebellar signs — and to convert a single attack into disease disseminated in time by mining the history for past events such as the year-old optic neuritis. MRI of the brain and whole spine then demonstrates dissemination in space (and, through simultaneous enhancing and non-enhancing lesions, in time), CSF oligoclonal bands or kappa free light chains lend support under the 2024 criteria, and a negative AQP4 retires NMOSD. The trap is anchoring on MS and skipping the cord MRI that excludes a treatable compressive lesion.

## Expert narrative
A young woman with a deficit evolving over days reads as demyelination almost immediately — but the experienced clinician's first safety move is to look for a sensory level and screen for back pain and sphincter loss, because a compressive cord lesion is the one mimic that cannot wait. Reassured by its absence, attention turns to assembling a multifocal CNS picture and, crucially, to the history: the year-old episode of painful visual loss is the dissemination-in-time that the vignette withheld. The examination harvests further separately-localising signs (an RAPD and pale disc, an internuclear ophthalmoplegia, cerebellar dysmetria), and MRI of the brain and whole spine then proves dissemination in space and time on a single study. CSF oligoclonal bands or kappa free light chains support the diagnosis under the 2024 McDonald criteria, while a negative AQP4 antibody specifically excludes NMOSD. The skill rewarded is holding the vascular, compressive and nutritional mimics live until each is retired — not collapsing onto MS because it fits the demographic.

## Sensitivity note
MRI of the brain and spine is the most sensitive test for demyelinating lesions and for demonstrating dissemination in space; CSF oligoclonal bands (or kappa free light chains) are sensitive supportive markers. The 2024 criteria increase early sensitivity by adding the optic nerve as a location and by relaxing the strict requirement for dissemination in time. History is the sensitive instrument for past attacks — they are easily missed if not specifically sought.

## Specificity note
CSF-restricted oligoclonal bands, an internuclear ophthalmoplegia and the combination of dissemination in space and time on MRI are relatively specific for MS in the right context. The central vein sign and paramagnetic rim lesions are highly specific newer markers. A negative AQP4 antibody specifically excludes NMOSD, and the absence of a sensory level argues specifically against a compressive cord lesion.
